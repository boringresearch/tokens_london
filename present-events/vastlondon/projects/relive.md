Project description
Relive: Your camera roll remembers everything. Now it can tell a story.
Meet your camera roll’s slightly dramatic sidekick.
You’ve got 14,000 photos and videos sitting in cloud purgatory. That trip to Tokyo. The birthday cake that became a fire drill. Forty minutes of cycling footage in which you were absolutely convinced something interesting happened.
Somewhere in there is a great story. Unfortunately, it’s surrounded by pavement.
Daystory turns “I should do something with these videos” into something worth watching.
Start with an idea:
“Make my bike ride feel like a nature documentary about a creature desperately trying to arrive at work on time.”
Daystory looks through the available footage, finds moments that support your idea, and drafts a storyboard with a beginning, a running joke, and a payoff. You review the scenes, change a line, swap a clip, or tell the narrator to take itself slightly less seriously.
Then the edit takes shape.
The uneventful stretch of road speeds past. A motorcycle enters the frame, and the film settles into normal speed. A tiny roadside detail gets a little breathing room—and a completely unnecessary amount of dramatic significance.
“Here we observe the commuter pursuing an increasingly endangered species: punctuality.”
“The motorcycle has already been promoted. Our subject remains under review.”
“Even the fire hydrant has a permanent position.”
The footage supplies the facts. The narrator supplies the attitude.
